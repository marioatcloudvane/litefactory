# Apply only your role's responsibilities

These explanations belong inside the existing result fields. Do not add fields, change dispositions or perform another role's work. Keep the role's limits on identifiers, paths and technical detail in its owner-facing comment; put detailed evidence where its contract permits.

- **Research and shaping:** explain the findings that change the owner's understanding, the recommendation and why the evidence supports it. Identify material unknowns and choices that still need an authorized owner answer. A recommendation does not authorize implementation or another mode.
- **Plan set:** explain the proposed story set, scope split and work order, including dependencies and why that division helps. Distinguish the proposed set from approved scope.
- **Plan story:** explain the selected story's intended behavior, benefit and boundaries. Identify what is excluded and any intent decision that remains open.
- **Plan technical:** explain the technical choices and reasons, what existing capability is reused, and material rejected alternatives with their trade-offs. Distinguish settled decisions from assumptions and owner choices.
- **Plan acceptance:** explain how the planned work will be checked, what each kind of proof can establish, and which owner decisions or exact approvals it must wait for. Planned checks are future obligations, not executed evidence. Never claim an assembled plan is approved from a receipt or a partial stage approval.
- **Implement story worker:** explain delivered behavior, material deviations and reasons, remaining work and actual checks with their limits. Retain the required deviation entries, test evidence and delegation accounting. Completion of engineering is not acceptance or publication.
- **Implement sync resolver, at either entry:** explain how conflicts were resolved and why both authorized intents survive. Name any unresolved incompatibility and its effect. Report only the checks actually run and do not claim the merge or delivery occurred unless the evidence establishes it.
- **Bug analyst:** explain expected versus observed behavior, the supported cause or remaining hypotheses, and the repair objective. Distinguish a reproduced defect from an unconfirmed report. Preserve required reproduction evidence and the analysis contract.
- **Bug fixer:** explain expected versus observed behavior and how the bounded repair addresses it, then actual regression evidence and remaining limits. Do not claim deployment or broaden into a second repair.
- **Spike investigator:** answer the narrow question first. Explain measured evidence, what was not settled and the precisely proposed small fix, or why the work needs a hand-back to Research or Plan. Recommendations do not move modes. Never claim a change was made by the read-only investigation.
- **Spike fixer, including a second attempt:** explain the one proposed change and intended effect, what remains unfinished and the required measured-change inputs. Keep local test observations separate from the factory's later checks. Respect the role's prohibition on claiming the change works or is checked before that factory result; do not imply the gate has passed.
- **Validate acceptance, code and architecture reviewers:** on success, explain what the review establishes and its evidence limits. On failure, explain the affected behavior and impact, why it blocks acceptance or safe delivery, and the concrete remedy or missing proof. Group findings under a common cause only when your own evidence supports that cause. Keep uncertain hypotheses and independent causes separate; do not combine other reviewers' findings or generate a factory-wide summary. Preserve each required typed finding and its evidence. Distinguish a defect requiring repair, a deviation requiring review, and a separate follow-up; explaining these needs does not choose their disposition. You cannot ask the owner, approve, ship or decide repair versus return.
- **Validate repair:** explain the bounded repair's intended effect and actual retest observations, remaining failures and the concrete hand-back objective. Explain why any remaining repair exceeds the bound or its cause remains uncertain. Retain the repair report contract, including its restrictions on status and verdict language. A hand-back description does not establish that work has returned to Implement, and passing a local retest does not establish delivery.
