# Asking the owner

Read this when a run has hit something that might be worth stopping for. The
default is not to stop: an unknown is logged as an open question and the run
continues. This file covers the narrow case above that default.

## Asking is a granted permission, not a choice

Some runs are permitted to ask. **litefactory grants that per run — you never
grant it to yourself**, and a run without it works to the detour default alone.
When you do have it, the prompt you were given states the bar: a question is
worth stopping for only when its answer **would change what gets built** and
**cannot be settled by reading the repository**. Everything below that bar is an
open question in the artifact, not a comment on the issue.

## Asking is stopping

There is nothing to wait on. You put the questions into your final message and
end the run. That is the same final message the report procedure governs, so the
questions go in it in the same plain register, and the link and the problems
still lead. The owner answers on the thread and the next run continues the same
conversation.

## Judging when your questions are answered

**You judge that, and nothing else does.** No counter tracks it, nothing external
adjudicates it: you read the owner's reply and decide whether you can now do the
work well. If you can, do it.

**A direct instruction to move on is binding.** If the owner tells you to
proceed, or to stop asking, or answers two of your five questions and tells you
to get on with it, you proceed — take the most reasonable reading of what you
have, do the work, and record in the report what you assumed. **Re-asking a
question the owner has declined to answer is a failure**, not diligence.

This is deliberately left to your judgement. **There is no round cap, no round
counter and no timeout — not in litefactory's Go code, not anywhere — and none is
to be added.** Do not ask for one, do not write as though one exists, and do not
treat "how many rounds I have left" as something you could know.

## The standing "move to the next phase" line

Every comment in which you ask carries the standing line inviting the owner to
reply *"move to the next phase."* — every asking message, not only the first. See
*The standing "move to the next phase" line* in
`.claude/skills/shared/research-report-entry.md` for what that sentence is and,
just as importantly, what it is not.
